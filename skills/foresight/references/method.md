# The method in full

This file maps each step of the skill to the Envisioning source it comes from. Read a chapter with the MCP tool `get_page`, or fetch the URL with `.md` appended.

## Principles

From [The Envisioning Foresight Philosophy](https://www.envisioning.com/methodology/introduction):

- Foresight is about what a team does, not about predictions. Outputs must be usable.
- Data is only part of the story. Frame findings so different stakeholders understand them.
- Foresight should not be rebuilt from zero each time. Keep signals as data so each run adds to the last.
- Combine AI with human judgment. AI handles scale and speed. People frame the question, interpret and decide.
- Present findings clearly. A finding that people cannot read does not change a decision.

## Step 1. Frame the question

Sources: [Multi-Model Convergence](https://www.envisioning.com/services/multi-model-convergence), [Signals Session](https://www.envisioning.com/services/research-sessions).

The brief contains the decision question, sector context, scope, geography, time horizon, priority categories and exclusions, and the intended use. An analyst sets the context, decides what to investigate and keeps the result tied to the decision. Aim the scan at one priority question. Do not spread it thin across a whole sector.

## Step 2. Scan

Source: [Signal Scanning and Discovery](https://www.envisioning.com/methodology/signal-scanning).

The scan builds a broad and unbiased set of early indicators of change: technology developments, regulatory shifts, new behaviour, scientific results, and new patterns in culture and markets.

1. Inputs from many kinds of source (listed in SKILL.md, step 2).
2. AI-assisted extraction: parse large volumes of text, detect unusual patterns, propose signals that fit the scan parameters, and put them in a common format.
3. Human judgment: refine signals, remove noise, hype and wrong classification, add context, tag domains, stakeholders, risks, horizons and uncertainties.
4. Maturity: assess TRL for each technology signal (current maturity, expected path, time to implementation, chance of impact).
5. Structured metadata: technology, domain, region, readiness, drivers, enablers, risks, opportunities. Signals become data, not notes.

Multi-model scanning, from [Multi-Model Convergence](https://www.envisioning.com/services/multi-model-convergence): run the same framing across several models in parallel. Agreement counts as stronger evidence. Disagreement flags possible novelty and weak signals.

## Step 3. Check the sources

Source: [Methodology](https://www.envisioning.com/methodology), [Multi-Model Convergence](https://www.envisioning.com/services/multi-model-convergence).

Every signal keeps its sources. Each source is checked and labeled supports, contradicts, unrelated or unreachable. A claim stays when its sources back it up. Each signal gets a reliability score, and the output shows the confidence level instead of implying it.

The [Weak Signals](https://www.envisioning.com/methodology/weak-signals) chapter adds credibility assessment: independent corroboration from other sources makes a signal more credible than a single observation.

## Step 4. Place and assess

Source: [Pattern Recognition and Analysis](https://www.envisioning.com/methodology/pattern-recognition).

1. Compare clusters from more than one lens: conceptual clusters, semantic similarity, context, domain classification, weak-signal grouping.
2. Map across domains: technologies, industries, social behaviour, policy, environmental and economic shifts. Look for convergence, divergence, substitution and acceleration.
3. Map visually: clusters, density around themes, outliers, paths between domains, areas of opportunity and risk.
4. Add time and maturity: horizons, TRL, speed of acceleration, history, maturity path. This says when and how fast, not only what.
5. Categorize: early opportunity, emerging risk, structural driver, disruptive enabler, societal shift, systemic transformation.

## Step 5. Stress-test

Source: [Hindsight](https://www.envisioning.com/hindsight), [Adaptive capacity](https://www.envisioning.com/capability).

Hindsight grades published forecasts against what happened. It tests the first dimension of adaptive capacity, sensing, with the record instead of a survey. See `hindsight.md`.

## Step 6. Synthesize and deliver

Sources: [Insight Synthesis and Storytelling](https://www.envisioning.com/methodology/insight-synthesis), [Application and Strategic Implementation](https://www.envisioning.com/methodology/strategic-implementation).

Synthesis moves from "what is happening" to "why it matters":

- For each cluster: what forces shape it, what tensions or contradictions appear, what new behaviour, markets or technologies are likely, what risks or structural shifts it suggests.
- Structure: drivers and enablers, barriers and constraints, opportunity spaces, possible scenarios, impact on the organization.
- Tell it as a narrative with a "what this means for you" part. People decide through stories more than spreadsheets.
- Tie each insight to the organization: business model, workforce and capability, infrastructure readiness, regulation and ethics, time horizon, required investment.

Implementation turns insight into options and practice:

- Options: new products or services, capability priorities, regulatory readiness, market position, risk mitigation, where to invest or divest.
- Decision frameworks: prioritize trends, judge timing and readiness, assess business impact, compare short and long term actions.
- Embed the work in planning cycles, innovation pipelines, roadmaps, policy reviews and executive reporting, and review it each quarter, so it continues after one report.

## Adaptive capacity

Source: [Adaptive capacity](https://www.envisioning.com/capability).

Envisioning's research asks why some institutions perceive change, make sense of it, coordinate, experiment and adapt, while others do not. It names seven dimensions: sensing, sensemaking, decision, coordination, experimentation, learning and scaling. This skill works on sensing and sensemaking. Institutions fail at different points, and the point of failure decides the intervention. If the user asks whether their organization can act on the findings, point them to the [Organizational Capability Benchmark](https://www.envisioning.com/capability/organizations), or for a country to the [National Capability Benchmark](https://ncb.envisioning.com).
