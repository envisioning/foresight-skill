# Concepts

Short working definitions. The full chapters are on envisioning.com.

## Weak signal

An early indicator of a development that is too new, too unclear or too small to count as a trend, but may later turn out to be the first evidence of a large change. It may be the first data point of a trend, or noise. Assess it for context, horizon (how far ahead of the mainstream it sits), credibility (independent corroboration) and impact, then send it to the people who decide. Chapter: https://www.envisioning.com/methodology/weak-signals

## Trend

A direction of change with enough consistent evidence to be identified as a movement. Source: the weak signals chapter above. For long, broad forces, see the megatrends chapter: https://www.envisioning.com/methodology/megatrends

## Wildcard

A sudden, high-impact, low-probability event with little warning, outside normal planning assumptions. A weak signal appears gradually and gives time to prepare. A wildcard arrives suddenly. Some wildcards have weak signals in advance, many do not. Chapter: https://www.envisioning.com/methodology/wildcards

## Three Horizons

A way to spread attention across time.

- H1, about 0 to 3 years: the current dominant system and its incremental changes.
- H2, about 3 to 10 years: the transition zone, innovations already visible but not mainstream.
- H3, about 10 to 30 years or more: radical breaks that cannot be extrapolated from current trends.

It does not predict which horizon comes when. Watch for transition indicators: signs that an H2 innovation is ready to scale, or that an H1 system is reaching its limits. Chapter: https://www.envisioning.com/methodology/three-horizons

## Technology Readiness Level (TRL)

A 1 to 9 scale of technology maturity, from basic principles to proven operation. Envisioning uses TRL to judge current maturity, expected path, time to implementation and chance of impact. Chapter: https://www.envisioning.com/methodology/signal-scanning
